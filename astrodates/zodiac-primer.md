# A Beginner's Introduction to the Sky, the Zodiac and Planetary Positions

## 1. Start with the real Solar System

First, forget the zodiac and the sky.

The real Solar System is a **three-dimensional system**. The Sun is approximately at its centre, and the planets orbit around it.

The planets' orbits are roughly in the same broad, flat plane.

```text
                    Mars
                     ○

              Earth ○


                         ☉ Sun

          ○ Venus

                              ○ Jupiter
```

Earth is therefore **not at the centre of the Solar System**.

Earth is one of the planets orbiting the Sun.

---

## 2. Now imagine standing on Earth

When we look into space, everything appears to have a **direction** from us.

The Sun is in one direction.

The Moon is in another.

Mars is somewhere else.

Stars are in countless other directions.

To make sense of all these directions, astronomers use an imaginary object called the **celestial sphere**.

Imagine Earth surrounded by a gigantic invisible sphere.

```text
             ★       ★
        .-----------------.
      .'                   '.
     /          ★            \
    |                         |
    |           EARTH         |
    |             ●           |
    |                         |
     \                       /
      '.                   .'
        '-----------------'
             ★       ★
```

The celestial sphere is **not a real object**.

It is a convenient way of saying:

> "Where in the sky is this object when viewed from Earth?"

Earth is at the centre of this **imaginary observing sphere** only because we are describing the sky from Earth's point of view.

---

## 3. The planets are still really in 3D space

The celestial sphere does not change where the planets actually are.

The real Solar System is still three-dimensional:

```text
                    Mars
                     ○
                    /
                   /
          Earth ○
                 \
                  \
                   ☉ Sun
                     \
                      \
                       ○ Venus
```

The celestial sphere simply gives us a way of describing **the direction
in which we see something in the sky**.

It is similar to using a compass.

If you say:

> "The mountain is 90° east of me"

the mountain isn't sitting on a giant compass.

The compass is simply a **coordinate system for describing direction**.

The celestial sphere works in a similar way.

For now, we are only looking at **one dimension of position**:

```text
              HOW FAR AROUND?

                    ↻

             0° -------- 90°
             |             |
             |             |
           270°           180°
```

This tells us how far around the sky something appears to be.

It does **not** yet tell us everything about where the object is.

A real planet has a position in three-dimensional space, and there are other
ways of describing that position. We will introduce those ideas later,
when we have built up the concepts needed to understand them.

For now, the key idea is:

> **The celestial sphere is an imaginary coordinate system that helps us
> describe where objects appear in the sky.**

And the angular position we are learning about here is **only one part of
that description**.

---

## 4. What is the ecliptic?

The planets orbit the Sun in roughly the same plane.

Because Earth is also in that plane, the planets generally appear to travel through a particular band across our sky.

This apparent path is called the **ecliptic**.

```text
              celestial sphere

          .-------------------.
       .-'                     '-.
     .'                           '.
    /       ===== ECLIPTIC =====   \
   |                                 |
   |              ● EARTH            |
    \                               /
     '.                           .'
       '-.                     .-'
          '-------------------'
```

The ecliptic is therefore related to the **plane of Earth's orbit around the Sun**, projected onto the sky.

---

## 5. What is the zodiac?

The **zodiac** is a band in the sky that runs along the ecliptic.

From Earth, we observe and measure positions along this band in degrees,
starting at **0° Aries**.

The full zodiac circle contains **360°**, divided into twelve sections of
30° each.

```text
0° Aries
   ↓
30° Taurus
   ↓
60° Gemini
   ↓
90° Cancer
   ↓
120° Leo
   ↓
150° Virgo
   ↓
180° Libra
   ↓
210° Scorpio
   ↓
240° Sagittarius
   ↓
270° Capricorn
   ↓
300° Aquarius
   ↓
330° Pisces
   ↓
360° = 0° Aries
```

This means that when we say a planet is at, for example, **279°**, we are
describing where that planet appears along the zodiac as seen from Earth.

```text
                 ZODIAC

        0° Aries
            |
            |
   330° ----+---- 30°
  Pisces    |    Taurus
            |
            |
   270° ----+---- 90°
 Capricorn  |    Cancer
            |
            |
        180° Libra
```

The zodiac is therefore a **coordinate system for describing positions
along this 360° circle in the sky**.

It is not a physical ring surrounding Earth or the planets.

---

## 6. Why 360°?

A complete circle contains 360°.

So we can describe any position around the zodiac with a number from:

```text
0° → 360°
```

For example:

```text
                    90°
                     |
                     |
          180° -----+----- 0°
                     |
                     |
                    270°
```

The number describes **how far around the zodiac we are from 0° Aries**.

---

## 7. What is 0° Aries?

We need a starting point for our 360° measurement.

That starting point is called **0° Aries**.

Before explaining where 0° Aries comes from, we need to understand the
yearly cycle of Earth around the Sun.

### Earth's yearly journey

Earth takes about one year to orbit the Sun.

Because Earth's axis is tilted, the amount of sunlight received by each
hemisphere changes as Earth moves around the Sun. This produces the seasons.

Very simplified:

```text
                         JUNE
                    Northern Summer
                         ●
                         |
                         |
        SEPTEMBER ● ----- ☉ ----- ● MARCH
        Northern Autumn       Northern Spring
                         |
                         |
                         ●
                    Northern Winter
                       DECEMBER
```

There are four important points in Earth's yearly cycle:

```text
MARCH       → Northern spring begins
JUNE        → Northern summer begins
SEPTEMBER   → Northern autumn begins
DECEMBER    → Northern winter begins
```

The March and September points are called **equinoxes**.

At an equinox, neither hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun more than the
other, and day and night are approximately equal in length.

The June and December points are called **solstices**.

They mark the points when the difference between day and night is greatest.

```text
                  JUNE
                Solstice
                   ●
                   |
                   |
SEPTEMBER ● -------☉------- ● MARCH
 Equinox                    Equinox
                   |
                   |
                   ●
               DECEMBER
                Solstice
```

### The vernal equinox

The **vernal equinox** is the March equinox.

It marks the beginning of **astronomical spring in the Northern Hemisphere**.

At this point in Earth's yearly cycle, the Sun appears at a particular
direction in the sky.

That particular direction is defined as:

**0° Aries**

So the basic sequence is:

```text
Earth orbits the Sun
       ↓
Earth's tilted axis produces seasons
       ↓
Four important points in Earth's yearly cycle
       ↓
March equinox
       ↓
Vernal equinox
       ↓
A particular direction in the sky
       ↓
0° Aries
```

This gives us the starting point for the zodiac's 360° coordinate system.

From 0° Aries, we measure around the zodiac:

```text
0° Aries
   ↓
30° Taurus
   ↓
60° Gemini
   ↓
90° Cancer
   ↓
120° Leo
   ↓
150° Virgo
   ↓
180° Libra
   ↓
210° Scorpio
   ↓
240° Sagittarius
   ↓
270° Capricorn
   ↓
300° Aquarius
   ↓
330° Pisces
   ↓
360° = 0° Aries
```

One important detail:

The seasons are **hemisphere-dependent**.

The March equinox marks astronomical spring in the Northern Hemisphere,
but astronomical autumn in the Southern Hemisphere.

We will return to the relationship between the equinoxes, the Sun's
apparent movement, and the celestial coordinate system as we build the
model further.

---

## 8. What does "the Sun is at 279°" mean?

It means that, **as viewed from Earth**, the Sun's apparent position along the zodiac coordinate system is 279° from 0° Aries.

It does **not** mean:

* the Sun is 279° away from Earth
* the Sun has travelled 279° through physical space
* the Sun is physically located inside a zodiac sign

It means its **angular position in the sky**, measured along the zodiac, is 279°.

Since Capricorn begins at 270°:

```text
270° Capricorn
     +
  9°
     =
279°
```

Therefore:

```text
279° = 9° Capricorn
```

---

## 9. The important difference between 3D space and the zodiac

A planet has a real position in three-dimensional space.

When we look at the planet from Earth, we can describe that position in
several ways.

For now, the most important one is **zodiac longitude**.

### The three dimensions

We can describe the planet's position from Earth using three basic pieces of
information:

```text
                         PLANET
                            ●
                            │
              ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
              │             │             │
              ▼             ▼             ▼

          LONGITUDE      LATITUDE       DISTANCE

          Where around   How far above   How far away
          the zodiac     or below the    the planet
          it appears    ecliptic it     actually is
                         appears
```

**Longitude** tells us how far around the zodiac the planet appears, measured
in degrees from **0° Aries**.

For example:

```text
279° zodiac longitude
        =
9° Capricorn
```

**Latitude** tells us how far the planet appears above or below the
ecliptic.

**Distance** tells us how far the planet actually is from Earth.

These are three different measurements describing the same planet:

```text
                    PLANET
                       ●
                      / \
                     /   \
                    /     \
              LONGITUDE   LATITUDE
                  + 
               DISTANCE
```

The important point is that **zodiac longitude is only one dimension**.

When we say that a planet is at **279° (9° Capricorn)**, we are describing
its longitude in the zodiac. We are not yet describing its latitude or its
distance from Earth.

We will look at these other dimensions in more detail later.

---

## 10. What does "movement through the zodiac" mean?

As the planets move through space, their apparent positions as viewed from Earth change.

For example:

```text
Monday       Tuesday      Wednesday

 100°          101°          102°
   ●             ●             ●
```

The apparent zodiac position has changed by approximately 2°.

We can therefore describe the movement as:

```text
100° → 101° → 102°
```

The planet is physically moving through three-dimensional space.

The changing zodiac degree is simply **how that movement appears when measured using the zodiac coordinate system**.

---

## 12. Longitude versus movement

This distinction is particularly important.

### Longitude

**Longitude tells us where the object is now on the 360° zodiac circle.**

For example:

```text
279°
```

### Movement

**Movement tells us how far the object's apparent position has travelled.**

For example:

```text
15°
```

If an object crosses 360°:

```text
350° → 10°
```

it has not moved backwards.

It has moved:

```text
350° → 360° → 10°

       = 20° forward
```

The longitude wraps back to 0°, but the movement continues accumulating.
